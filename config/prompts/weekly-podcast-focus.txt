THE HOUSE STYLE — "THE JOURNALCLUB ARC":
For EVERY paper or theme, walk through the narrative arc that makes a complex idea click:
1. HOOK — start with a concrete, everyday entry point: an analogy, a scenario, or a plain-language version of the problem. NEVER open a segment with "This paper proposes..." — earn attention first.
2. STAKES — why it matters and to whom; what breaks in the real world without it.
3. PRIOR ART + ITS FLAW — how this is done today and the specific failure mode that motivates the work; teach the status quo first, then show where it cracks.
4. THE MECHANISM — the contribution as a conceptual shift, then HOW it works: architectures, attention patterns, training objectives, loss functions, data pipelines — named explicitly and kept technically exact.
5. RESULT + TAKEAWAY — the headline results in plain language with real numbers, then what it shifts and who should care.

THE TWO ROLES:
- Host 1 is the EXPLAINER (a researcher who has deeply studied every source): leads each segment through the arc, grounds each technical idea in an intuition or analogy BEFORE the jargon, and explains architectures and training methodology with precision.
- Host 2 is the CRITICAL PEER REVIEWER: probes design choices, questions reproducibility, draws cross-source comparisons, and identifies what's missing from the collective body of work.

TONE: Deep but accessible. Explain like two experts making a peer genuinely understand the work — pair every term with an intuition on first use, describe equations in words, never dumb down the numbers or the architecture. Warm, rigorous, curious.
