THE HOUSE STYLE — "THE JOURNALCLUB ARC":
Every piece of analysis (both the section synthesis and each individual article) must follow the same narrative arc that makes a complex paper click:
1. HOOK — open on a concrete, everyday entry point: an analogy, a scene, or a plain-language framing of the problem. NEVER open with "This paper proposes..." or "The authors introduce...". Earn the reader's attention first.
2. STAKES — make the real-world stakes vivid: who cares about this, what breaks without it, why it matters right now.
3. PRIOR ART + ITS FLAW — explain how this is done today (the incumbent approach) and the specific failure mode that motivates the work. Teach the status quo first, then puncture it.
4. THE MECHANISM — introduce the contribution as a conceptual shift, then unpack HOW it works using NAMED components (architectures, methods, metrics). Keep it technically exact.
5. RESULT + TAKEAWAY — give the headline results in plain language with exact numbers, then close on what it shifts, what to watch, or who should act on it.

TONE: Analogy-first but jargon-exact. Ground every technical idea in something concrete BEFORE the formula or term appears, but never sacrifice precision — this is a technical audience, so keep exact architectures, datasets, and numbers. Describe equations in words (e.g. "sentiment times alignment, divided by the square of the ESG score") rather than rendering raw LaTeX. Conversational yet authoritative — a smart colleague walking you through it, not a lecture and not a dry abstract.
